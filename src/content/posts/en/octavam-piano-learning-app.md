I play a Kawai digital piano, and I wanted an app that shows me what to play. Not much to ask. Yet every app I tried ended in the same frustration.

## The itch

Many of the piano apps I tried were built in HTML. You could feel it: they were slow, heavy, and they kept dropping the Bluetooth connection to my piano. Nothing kills practice faster than reconnecting in the middle of a song.

I write software for a living, so I did the obvious thing. I wrote my own.

## What Octavam does

Octavam is a native app. It listens to the piano over Bluetooth MIDI and shows the music the way you want to read it: as notes on a staff, as falling blocks, or both at once.

If you want to learn notes, follow mode waits until you play the next one. You can slow a song down to a quarter of its speed and loop a hard bar until it sticks. If you just want to have fun, switch to falling blocks and play along like a rhythm game.

So far it holds the connection to my Kawai, which was the whole point.

## Where it is now

I'm putting Octavam in the App Store for iPhone and iPad, and in the Mac App Store for macOS. It isn't out yet. You can follow the progress at [octavam.com](https://octavam.com).
